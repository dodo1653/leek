Satoshi's Coins — The Quantum Treasure

~1,100,000 BTC · MINED 2009-2010 · NEVER MOVED

The most famous coins in Bitcoin history. Mined by Satoshi Nakamoto in the earliest days of the network, these ~1.1 million BTC have never moved. Their public keys are exposed on the blockchain — a permanent target for any entity that achieves quantum supremacy. Whoever claims Satoshi's coins does not just steal money — they seize the origin story of Bitcoin itself.

P2PK Addresses — The Original Exposure

EARLY MINERS · PAY-TO-PUBLIC-KEY · FULLY EXPOSED

Before BIP-16 (P2SH) and BIP-141 (SegWit), Bitcoin used Pay-to-Public-Key (P2PK) addresses. These reveal the full public key in every transaction. Satoshi's coins and early miner holdings use P2PK — the most quantum-vulnerable format in existence. These are not theoretical risks; they are sitting ducks.

Reused Addresses — The Double Spend Risk

MILLIONS OF ADDRESSES · PUBLIC KEY VISIBLE AFTER FIRST SPEND

When a Bitcoin address receives funds, the public key is hidden behind a hash. But when you spend from that address, the public key is revealed on-chain. Any address that has ever sent a transaction has a visible public key. If that address has also received new funds, those funds are vulnerable. Millions of Bitcoin users are exposed without knowing it.

BIP-360 — The Permanent Fix

BITCOIN IMPROVEMENT PROPOSAL · HASH-BASED SIGNATURES

BIP-360 defines the technical standard for quantum-resistant signatures on Bitcoin. By replacing elliptic curve cryptography with hash-based signatures, BIP-360 provides a backward-compatible path to quantum safety. The $15M consortium — Coinbase, BlackRock, Fidelity, Galaxy, Strategy, and Blockstream — is backing its implementation. This is Bitcoin's immune system against quantum attack.
